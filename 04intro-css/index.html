About Guinea Pigs

What is a Cavy?

Guinea pigs, scientifically classified as Cavia porcellus, are friendly domesticated rodents originally native to the Andes region of South America. Despite their common name, they do not originate from Guinea and are not biologically related to pigs. These charming herbivores are widely adored as family pets due to their gentle temperament, unique vocalizations, and highly social nature.

Cavies thrive when kept in pairs or small bonded groups because social isolation causes them severe distress. In addition to regular interaction, guinea pigs require large flat-bottom cages, an unlimited supply of high-grade timothy hay, fresh daily vegetables, and supplemented Vitamin C, as their bodies cannot synthesize this vital nutrient on their own.

Purpose of This Site

The goal of Guinea Pig Breeds 101 is to educate current and prospective pet parents on the wide variety of recognized breeds across the globe. From sleek short-coated varieties to flowing long-haired types and unique hairless companions, understanding breed characteristics ensures you can provide the ideal grooming routine, habitat, and dietary care tailored to your pet's needs.

Learn more about responsible pet ownership at the Humane Society or explore breed standards through the American Rabbit Breeders Association.

Essential Daily Care Steps

  1. Replenish fresh timothy hay to promote proper digestion and wear down constantly growing teeth.
  2. Provide clean, fresh water in an upright sipper bottle or heavy ceramic crock.
  3. Feed measured plain guinea pig pellets fortified with stable Vitamin C.
  4. Offer a cup of rinsed leafy greens such as green leaf lettuce, cilantro, or bell peppers.

Common Cavy Terminology

Boar
An intact adult male guinea pig.
Sow
An adult female guinea pig.
Pup
A baby guinea pig born fully furred with eyes open and teeth intact.
